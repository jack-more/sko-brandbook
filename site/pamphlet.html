The part that actually matters

You are the story

Over thirty videos you are not making thirty ads. You are letting people watch you be into something — how you got here, what you got wrong early on, what you look for now.

Nobody follows a creator for a product pitch. They follow a person. The videos that work for us are the ones where someone is clearly talking about their own life and the product happens to be in it — not the ones where a script is being performed at a camera.

So be candid. Say the thing you would say to a friend, including the unflattering parts. "I spent six months buying from whoever was cheapest and I regret it" is a better video than anything we could write for you. Your skepticism is interesting. Your standards are interesting. The reason you care is interesting.

Tell the story of
how you got here.

One thing worth knowing as you shape it: keep the story about you rather than about what a compound did to your body. Not because we are policing you — because "here is what I research and why" is a story you can tell thirty different ways, and "here is what it did to me" is one video that also tends to get pulled.

Two things we do ask for in every video, and they are the only two: the product on screen somewhere, and your code, said out loud and put on screen. Everything between those is yours.

Week one

Five swings, five shapes

We do not know what works on your page and neither do you until you try. Make one of each, in your own editing style, then we keep whatever landed. Just do not make the same video five times with different backgrounds.

SHAPE ONE

Talking to camera

You, your room, no edit tricks. Not the lazy option — the best-performing videos we have are exactly this, one person talking straight into the lens.

SHAPE TWO

In hand / unboxing

The box arrives, you open it, you talk through what you are looking at. The easiest one to make convincing.

SHAPE THREE

In your routine

Where it actually sits in your day. Shot like the rest of your day-in-the-life content, not like a commercial.

SHAPE FOUR

Street or reaction

You asking someone else what they think, or reacting to something on screen. Works when the other person's reaction is real.

SHAPE FIVE

Your own format

Whatever already performs on your page. Honestly, this is the one we are most interested in — if it works we would rather learn it from you than talk you out of it.

Only if you want it

If a video isn't landing

This is not the shape of every video and you should not work through it like a checklist — plenty of good videos use two of these. It is a list of what tends to be missing when something flopped. Read it when one does, ignore it the rest of the time.

01
Problem Something a viewer already deals with, early. Use one you actually have — an invented problem reads as an ad instantly.
02
Statistic A real number. The only one we can give you is 40,000+ orders shipped. Skip it rather than invent one.
03
FOMO What they are missing by putting it off. Not a fake deadline — the actual cost of waiting.
04
Self-identification The line that makes the right viewer realise the video is for them.
05
Product discovery How you came across SKO. Better as a story than a handoff into an ad read.
06
Objection Their doubt, said out loud before they think it. Whatever made you hesitate is making them hesitate.
07
Visual proof Show it. Vial in your hand, the box, the label, your order screen. Talking about something you never show is the most common reason a video does not convert.
08
Social normalization Other people already do this. It stops being a risk and starts being a thing people do.
09
Credibility Whatever you can say honestly. Ours are third-party tested, Beverly Hills based, 40,000+ orders shipped.
10
Simplicity How easy it actually was. Most people assume it is complicated and stop there.
11
Call to action Your code, 10% off, where to go. Out loud and on screen — this is the one people forget.
Under 25 seconds there is not room for most of this. Something that hooks, something shown, and your code is usually the whole video.

Worth knowing

What gets videos pulled

None of this is house style or us policing your voice — be as blunt as you like. It is the short list of things TikTok and the regulators actually act on, so a good video doesn't cost you a page you spent years building.

Telling people what to take, or how much. Dosing, protocols, "I take this for that" — it reads as medical advice and it is the quickest way to lose a video. Talking about your research, your reasoning and your standards is fine.
Before-and-afters and results claims. Body transformation shots get pulled, and they tend to put the account at risk rather than just the post.
Weight-loss language. Flagged automatically, including the brand names in that category. Worth avoiding in captions too.
Purity percentages. "Third-party tested" holds up; a specific percentage is a claim someone has to defend. The number printed on the physical label is part of the product and can be on camera — just don't read it out.
Bought comments or engagement groups. This one risks the account itself rather than the video.
Removing TikTok's AI label. If you used AI anywhere in the edit, leaving it on costs you nothing and taking it off gets accounts actioned.
A few products aren't cleared for ads. Naming these usually brings the video down: SKO-3 RT, SKO-TRZ, Cagrilintide, Metabolic Reference Bundle, AOD-9604, MOTS-c, 5-Amino-1MQ, TB-500, Wolverine Blend.
If something feels borderline, put it in the group before you post — nobody is going to be annoyed. And if a video does come down, tell us what the notice said so everyone else can avoid it.
