The idea

Three materials, three roles. The blue is a pigment: dry, matte, absolute, something you pour, dust and carve. The chrome is a body: liquid mirror, cast as a hand, a figure, a ribbon, never a person. The vial is the only subject, the only thing that is ever photographed.

Power comes from stillness. One big object per frame, hard light, flat ground. Not from more objects, more colours or more motion. If a frame has two subjects it has none.

Cold, not cute. Snow, water, ice, stone, marble. Alpine air. Nothing warm, nothing soft, nothing that could sell a sports drink.

The site follows the book. One object per screen on flat Pigment, heavy type under it, nothing else.

SKO BRAND BOOKIDENTITY

Colour

One pigment, lit three ways. Pigment is the ground and it is International Klein Blue as it photographs on screen. Ink is that same blue in shadow, the deep of the water. Sky is it under hard light. Frost is the one pale blue, taken from lit snow, and it is a highlight, never a surface.

Chrome is not a colour. It is white to Frost with Pigment in its shadows. Any chrome that does not reflect the blue is wrong.

Paper is the rest. Off-white, for the rock shots and the pages between blue ones. Pure white is never used as a ground.

The pack keeps #173384. It is a print measurement, not a screen token.

Type

Heavy and plain. Archivo Black is the display face, set big and left, white on Pigment. It is Helvetica Bold's weight without Helvetica's licence. No tracking, no outlines, no gradients, no italics.

Inter carries everything that has to be read: body, captions, labels, the site.

JetBrains Mono is the hood. Readouts, batch numbers, the Array, anything that is data. It is the only place the brand is allowed to look like a machine.

Display is never smaller than 56 px on a 350 px slot. If it has to be small, it is Inter.

SKO BRAND BOOKTYPE

The grounds

Four grounds, one blue. Pigment powder, blue water, blue-lit snow, and Paper with a pigment rock. Every product shot stands on one of these. Nothing else is a ground.

Pigment is the cover and the primary. The vial lies in it or stands on it, dust on the cap, label clean.

Water is hard sun, caustics, a broken reflection. Snow is dusk, blue light, crystals at the base. Paper is the only light ground, and the pigment always comes with it.

Wet stays optional. The water ground is the one place it is expected.

The body

Chrome all the way. A hand, a figure, a ribbon. Solid liquid mirror, no skin anywhere, no glove edge, no face. The chrome reflects Pigment in its shadows and goes white in its highlights.

The body holds the vial. It never competes with it. If the chrome has its own glow, grid or highlight rig, it has become a second subject and the frame is wrong.

No candid photography, no statues, no figures. People appear as a chrome hand, or not at all.

The dark set

For ads, not for the book. Three frames where the blue becomes a light instead of a pigment: the liquid glowing from inside the glass, the vial frozen into ice, the vial on a ledge of a pigment cliff.

They are louder than the grounds and they earn their place in paid media, where a frame has half a second. On the site they are used once, if at all.

The glow is the liquid, never a halo behind the vial.

The hood

Where cyber lives. The brand's technical edge is under the surface: mono readouts, the Array, batch data. It is text and data, never imagery. No HUD lines, no grids, no neon, no warp.

Ink ground, Frost text, JetBrains Mono. It reads like an instrument panel and it is the only place the brand is allowed to.
