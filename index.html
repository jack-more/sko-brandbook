Logo

Three shapes, and the names matter because the filename is what the next person trusts.

3×1 is the horizontal lockup — shield left, wordmark right. It is the default wherever there is width for it.

1×1 is the stacked lockup — shield on top, SKO COMPOUNDS beneath. Roughly square. This is the app icon and the avatar.

SHIELD is the mark on its own, taller than wide. It is not the 1×1 — the 1×1 still carries the wordmark.

Clear space on all four sides equals the height of the shield. White on coloured grounds and photography, navy on light. Disclaimers always take the flat white shield.

The Flat Shield

Two cuts, and they are not interchangeable.

PLAIN is the solid silhouette — no helix, nothing inside it. It is the badge: reach for it to contain something, to sit over a photograph, or anywhere the mark would be too busy to read.

MARK is the shield with the helix knocked out. Because the helix is a hole, it takes the colour of whatever is behind it — which is right on a flat ground and wrong on a busy one. On a photograph, use PLAIN.

Both are one colour, cut from the vector: no gradient, no bevel. White for coloured grounds and every disclaimer; blue and deep for light grounds.

Below roughly 48 px, and over any image, these are the only cuts. The full‑colour mark is a render and does not survive either.

Colour

One blue at three values. SKO Blue is the primary, measured off the label. Field is the same blue held brighter — what blue becomes when blue is the whole surface. Foil is a material, not a colour: two bands on the label, and it appears only where it physically exists.

The blue is already Yves Klein Blue. IKB is #002FA7, hue 223. SKO Blue is #173384, hue 224 — the same hue, ours darker. Nothing to chase. But Klein’s blue was never a backdrop, it was the work, and that is the rule we take.

Measured 29 Aug 2026 off the label artwork. #26379B, #1B2A7A and the store’s #1447D6 were read off photographs, not the pack, and are retired.

Motion

The glitch DNA transition is the brand’s own move, read off the reference frame by frame at 24fps. These are the values the build uses — running, not described.

Hard cuts only. Every state change happens in a single frame. There are no crossfades anywhere in the system: a dissolve reads as a transition effect, a cut reads as a machine switching modes.

Two clocks, never locked. Structure holds 300–720ms; accretion toggles every 60–320ms on its own timer. The flicker between them is where the life is.

The flash is a cut, not a glow. One frame of flat blue, about 42ms, roughly one state change in six.

The Array

The catalogue in orbit — all 34 units, with the names, sizes and prices read off the live store. Every unit is a real vial in three dimensions, not a picture of one.

The profile is that SKU’s own silhouette, measured off its catalogue render and revolved, so the body, shoulder, neck, crimp collar and flip‑top are the shape the product actually is. The label is its own artwork, un‑projected off the front‑on render and wrapped. Units are held at one height the way the rail shot holds them, and diameter is left alone — so a tall vial reads correctly as a thin one.

At the centre, the helix runs the full height and out both ends — the catalogue orbits it rather than the helix sitting among the units. Liquid metal carrying real thin‑film iridescence, the same physics as the foil on the label. Strands offset 0.42 of a turn, pitch held near twice the diameter so it reads as DNA and not as a spring.

The ground is Field #244399, flat. No gradient, no vignette. The depth in the frame comes from the objects in it, never from the surface behind them.

Drag to spin it. It carries momentum and settles. Hovering holds it still and raises that unit; clicking opens that product.

Helix

The connective asset. It is never drawn as line art — what is on the page is what an inspection system leaves behind looking at a helix: points, connections, brackets, labels, tracking boxes, and selective focus.

The geometry stays honest. Strands are offset 0.42 of a turn, not half, which is what gives B‑DNA its wide major groove and narrow minor one. Depth drives size, brightness and draw order, so the near strand occludes the far one.

The path is constant, the substance is not. Six states — inspection, accretion, ladder, chain, readout, disperse — and it tears between them on a seven-band horizontal glitch. The silhouette never breaks.

Three scales ship alongside: hero, frame, and a field carrying no boxes or labels that runs under other work.

Helix — the cuts

One helix, six treatments. The geometry is identical in all six — strands offset 0.42 of a turn — so the only variable is material.

Three are rendered. Liquid metal is the hero cut and carries real thin‑film iridescence, the same physics as the foil. White and matte blue are the quiet ones.

Three are flat. Unlit colour, no shading, no highlight. These are the cuts that survive small sizes, flat print and embroidery — and they are the default wherever a render cannot be trusted to hold.

And one that is the mark itself. The logo ombré runs the lockup’s own range down the strand — the light chrome‑blue of the helix at the top, through the palette, to the deep navy the shield ends on at its point. Both ends are sampled off the artwork.

Each sits on the ground it is for: white on blue, blue on paper, metal and light blue on carbon.

Alternates — not in use

None of these ship. They are here so the set is documented and so nobody finds one in an old folder and assumes it is live.

Every one of them is a render — embossed, distressed, textured. That treatment reads as playful, and it is the opposite of what this brand is for. It also does not survive being shrunk or laid over an image.

If you have one of these in a working file, replace it with a flat cut from the vector.

Kept for reference only. Do not place these in new work.

Photography

Four approved sets. Every one is the real product, composited — never generated. A generated vial invents a new bottle each time: wrong proportions, a label carrying no SKU, foil bands that come and go.

The ground is always from the palette — SKO Blue, Paper, Halo or Deep. The light is always one source, high and to the left. The product is never treated: nothing crosses the vial, no shadow falls on the label, the bottle is never warped.

Structure lives behind the product or beside it, never over it.
