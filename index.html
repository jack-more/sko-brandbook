Logo

The lockup is used in white on navy and on photography, and in navy on light grounds. Give it clear space equal to the height of the shield on all four sides.

Below the minimum width the rules either side of COMPOUNDS close up — drop to the 1×1.

The 1×1 is the mark on its own, square, and it is the version that already ships: it is the site’s app icon and the embroidery on the black Columbia Slack Tide. Silver is the apparel cut; navy and white are the print and screen cuts. Full colour is the supplied artwork and is only for app icons and favicons — never for print.

Disclaimers always take the flat white shield. Research Use Only, the claim strip, the legal line at the foot of an ad — the mark beside them is the flat white cut. Never the full‑colour one, never silver.

Small and overlaid, go flat. The full‑colour mark is a render — gradient shield, chrome helix, bevelled wordmark — and none of that survives being shrunk or laid over a photograph. Below roughly 48 px, and anywhere the mark sits on an image, use flat matte blue or flat matte white, whichever holds against the ground. Both come off the vector: one colour, no gradient, no bevel.

Colour

One blue at three values. SKO Blue is the primary, measured off the label. Field is the same blue held brighter — what blue becomes when blue is the whole surface. Foil is a material, not a colour: two bands on the label, and it appears only where it physically exists.

The blue is already Yves Klein Blue. IKB is #002FA7, hue 223. SKO Blue is #173384, hue 224 — the same hue, ours darker. Nothing to chase. But Klein’s blue was never a backdrop, it was the work, and that is the rule we take.

Measured 29 Aug 2026 off the label artwork. #26379B, #1B2A7A and the store’s #1447D6 were read off photographs, not the pack, and are retired.

The Array

The catalogue in orbit — all 34 units, with the names, sizes and prices read off the live store. Every unit is a real vial in three dimensions, not a picture of one.

The profile is that SKU’s own silhouette, measured off its catalogue render and revolved, so the body, shoulder, neck, crimp collar and flip‑top are the shape the product actually is. The label is its own artwork, un‑projected off the front‑on render and wrapped. Units are held at one height the way the rail shot holds them, and diameter is left alone — so a tall vial reads correctly as a thin one.

At the centre, a machined core carrying real thin‑film iridescence — the same physics as the foil on the label — with a light blue fresnel bleeding off its edge.

The ground is Field #244399, flat. No gradient, no vignette. The depth in the frame comes from the objects in it, never from the surface behind them.

Helix

The connective asset. It is never drawn as line art — what is on the page is what an inspection system leaves behind looking at a helix: points, connections, brackets, labels, tracking boxes, and selective focus.

The geometry stays honest. Strands are offset 0.42 of a turn, not half, which is what gives B‑DNA its wide major groove and narrow minor one. Depth drives size, brightness and draw order, so the near strand occludes the far one.

The path is constant, the substance is not. Six states — inspection, accretion, ladder, chain, readout, disperse — and it tears between them on a seven-band horizontal glitch. The silhouette never breaks.

Three scales ship alongside: hero, frame, and a field carrying no boxes or labels that runs under other work.

Helix — the cuts

One helix, six treatments. The geometry is identical in all six — strands offset 0.42 of a turn — so the only variable is material.

Three are rendered. Liquid metal is the hero cut and carries real thin‑film iridescence, the same physics as the foil. White and matte blue are the quiet ones.

Three are flat. Unlit colour, no shading, no highlight. These are the cuts that survive small sizes, flat print and embroidery — and they are the default wherever a render cannot be trusted to hold.

Each sits on the ground it is for: white on blue, blue on paper, metal and light blue on carbon.

Photography

Four approved sets. Every one is the real product, composited — never generated. A generated vial invents a new bottle each time: wrong proportions, a label carrying no SKU, foil bands that come and go.

The ground is always from the palette — SKO Blue, Paper, Halo or Deep. The light is always one source, high and to the left. The product is never treated: nothing crosses the vial, no shadow falls on the label, the bottle is never warped.

Structure lives behind the product or beside it, never over it.
